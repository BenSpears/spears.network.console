I mentioned this in passing in an [earlier post](/posts/why-i-left-grapheneos-for-an-iphone/):
sign into a Google account and it keeps a running log of what you do — searches,
YouTube history, location, if you let it — sitting in a dashboard most people
never open. That line was true enough to be worth its own post, because it's not
just Google. Every major account you use keeps one of these, and the settings to
turn it down are already sitting there, not locked behind some future privacy law
that might pass.

## Every account already has this dashboard

This isn't a hidden feature. It's usually one menu deep, under a name like
"Activity," "Data & Privacy," or "Privacy Checkup." The reason almost nobody's
seen it isn't that it's hard to find — it's that nothing ever prompts you to
look. You set the account up once, clicked through the defaults, and never had a
reason to go back. The dashboards below take about five minutes each once you
know where they are.

## Google: what's actually being logged

Go to myactivity.google.com. This is the big one — by default it logs your
searches, the sites you visit through Chrome if you're signed in, your YouTube
watch and search history, and your location history if it's on. Turn off what you
don't want tracked under Web & App Activity, Location History, and YouTube
History, and set auto-delete to three months on whatever you keep instead of
leaving it to accumulate forever. Separately, check myadcenter.google.com and
turn off ad personalization — that's the profile built specifically to target ads
at you, and it's a different setting from the activity log itself.

## Apple: a smaller ledger, still worth checking

Apple's business model doesn't run on your data the way Google's does, and it
shows — there's less here. Still worth five minutes: Settings → Privacy &
Security → Analytics & Improvements, where you can turn off sharing iPhone
Analytics and Improve Health & Activity. And under Settings → Privacy & Security
→ Apple Advertising, turn off Personalized Ads. Smaller ledger, but no reason to
leave any of it on.

## Meta, if you still use it

If you're still on Facebook or Instagram, Meta keeps one of the most detailed
activity logs of any platform — it tracks your activity across a huge number of
third-party sites and apps that use Meta's ad tools, whether you're actively
using Facebook at that moment or not. In Settings → Accounts Center → Your
information and permissions → Off-Facebook activity, you can see what's been
reported and clear the history. It's worth doing even if you rarely open the app;
the tracking doesn't require you to.

## Do it this week

1. Google: myactivity.google.com — turn off what you don't need, auto-delete the rest.
2. Google: myadcenter.google.com — turn off ad personalization.
3. Apple: turn off Analytics sharing and Personalized Ads.
4. Meta, if applicable: clear Off-Facebook activity and check what's still connected.

None of this requires a new tool or a subscription. It's settings that already
exist, in accounts you already have — the only thing missing was a reason to go
look.
