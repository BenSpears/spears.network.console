Most of the spam you get isn't random. Your name, address, phone number, and a
list of your relatives are sitting on dozens of people-search sites, assembled
from public records and purchased data, and sold to whoever wants them. That's
where a lot of the robocalls, spam texts, and junk mail come from. It's also the
raw material for anyone trying to figure out where you live.

You can opt out of these sites yourself. The [Big-Ass Data Broker Opt-Out List](https://github.com/yaelwrites/Big-Ass-Data-Broker-Opt-Out-List)
is a solid, free place to start, with direct links to the removal pages. I've
done it the manual way. It works, but it's tedious, every site has its own
process, and they quietly repopulate your data after a few months, so it's never
actually finished.

So I pay for [EasyOptOuts](https://easyoptouts.com/) instead. It's
about $20 a year. You give it your details, it checks a couple hundred
people-search sites, files the removals for you, and re-runs the whole thing
every four months so the entries that creep back get taken down again. [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics/personal-information/services-that-delete-data-from-people-search-sites-review-a2705843415/)
rated it the most effective of the single-subscription services they tested, and
it's also the cheapest, which is why [Privacy Guides](https://www.privacyguides.org/en/data-broker-removals/)
and others land on it too.

The honest result: since I signed up, my spam calls, texts, and junk email have
dropped noticeably. Not to zero, but enough that I noticed.

A few things worth being straight about. You do have to hand a third party your
real information so it can find you. That feels backwards, but there's no way to
request a removal without proving who you are. It isn't instant either; the sites
are slow, and removal runs maybe two-thirds effective, not perfect. And because
the data comes back, this is a subscription, not a one-time cleanup.

This is one of the highest-return privacy moves I know of for the effort
involved: twenty dollars and ten minutes, and a lot less noise on your phone.
Most privacy advice asks you to change how you live. This one just quietly
deletes you from a market you never agreed to join.
